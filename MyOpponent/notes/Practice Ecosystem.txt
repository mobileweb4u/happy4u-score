Here is the comprehensive architectural summary and deployment guide to implement the Practice Night Statistics Ledger for Version 4.5.4 of your application.

1. Understanding the App's Practice Ecosystem
Based on menu.js, choosing "Practice Night" activates a specific format with a base target score (raceInput.value = 5) designed for training or casual club play. However, unlike strict league fixtures, a true practice session at the table is continuous.

Instead of exiting the application when a single race concludes, players want to run consecutive sub-races (Game 1, Game 2, Game 3...) back-to-back. To track performance accurately over an entire evening, your data structure must record each individual milestone leg while maintaining a single, unified text transcript log.

2. The Best Architectural Solution
The absolute best approach for your app is to separate your tournament data from your practice records by introducing a dedicated local storage collection key: practice_night_history. This guarantees that casual training metrics never mix with or corrupt your competitive championship league arrays.

The Data Collection Pipeline
The Frame Monitor: Your main scoring loop monitors live points. The exact millisecond either player hits 5 frames, a snapshot of that specific leg (e.g., 5-2, 5-1) is appended to practice_night_history.

The Session Counter (* X): Instead of a fixed text label, the report engine tracks how many total sets of 5 were completed during the night. If you reset and play 3 complete sets, the header updates dynamically to read: TYPE: STANDARD RACE TO 5 * 3.

The Non-Blocking Reset: After a 5-frame leg is captured, the app clears the on-screen scores back to 0-0 instantly, tracking overall session totals and allowing continuous play without disrupting the flow at the table.
